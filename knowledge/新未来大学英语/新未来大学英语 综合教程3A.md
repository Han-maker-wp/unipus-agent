# Unit 1 CAREER PLANNING

## Section 1 · Episode 1 · Video scripts

Mr. Clay: Come in, ah, I've been expecting you.
Yi Fei: Hi, Mr. Clay.
Mr. Clay: Hello.
Tom: Thanks for seeing us.
Mr. Clay: No problem.

Mr. Clay: So you are here to ask for some advice on career planning, right?
Tom: That's right.
Mr. Clay: OK. Now the purpose of career planning is to map out how you want to progress your career. So, tell me, what're your career goals?
Tom: That's easy. I'm interested in marketing, so I want to become a CMO. And I want to become a data scientist.
Mr. Clay: Right. Oh, those are certainly very admirable ambitions, but how do you know that those positions are right for you?
Tom: Umm, I'm not sure I understand.
Mr. Clay: Right. Have either of you done a self-analysis?
Yi Fei: You mean reflecting on our personal strengths and weaknesses?
Mr. Clay: That's certainly one aspect, but not all by any means.

Mr. Clay: Thinking of our strengths and weaknesses, as you said, is a very good starting point. But it is also vital that we are brutally honest with ourselves about our personalities.
Yi Fei: How can we do that?
Mr. Clay: Well, it can be good to talk to other people who know you to see if they have the same ideas about you. For example, Yi Fei, you may say that you have a lot of confidence, but others....
Tom: May see this as arrogance?
Mr. Clay: Exactly.
Yi Fei: Oh, I see.
Tom: Why are these things important when we're planning our career path?
Mr. Clay: Thinking about what knowledge, skills, experiences, and personal characteristics we have can help us to see if we're a good fit for any particular job. Also, it's very important that we know our real interests and work place preferences. Once you have finished your self-analysis, then there are three steps to take. The first step is to identify career goals and jobs that you think suit you based on your self-analysis.
Tom: Oh, so we shouldn't decide what job we want until after we do the self-analysis?
Mr. Clay: You shouldn't make your career options too narrow at this stage, as you may want to give yourself several possibilities. Actually, a career aptitude test might well throw up some unexpected options for you to consider.
Yi Fei: Wow! I hadn't thought of that.
Mr. Clay: The second step is to learn about your preferred career options. Collect information such as industry trend reports or job descriptions. You can also interview someone who works in the occupation that interests you.
Yi Fei: Then we can eliminate the jobs that don't appeal to us and decide on the best fit!
Mr. Clay: Absolutely. The third and final step is to write your career plan as a guide to reaching your ultimate career goal. You can break it down into long-term and short-term goals, and then come up with an action plan.
Yi Fei: I see.
Tom: That's very helpful. Thank you very much!

**Yi Fei**: I saw a job advert for the role of a research intern with a small tech company and I'm thinking of applying for it. I was wondering if I could ask you for some advice about what to include in my resume.

**Mr. Clay**: Of course, Yi Fei. Let's start with the basics. You need to include your name, contact details, your education and any achievements like awards or prizes ...

**Yi Fei**: OK. I have consistently been in the top 1 percent of my class ... Ah, I also came second in a national science contest last year.

**Mr. Clay**: Perfect! Oh! Don't forget to include any work experience that you have.

**Yi Fei**: Well, I don't really have any, which is one reason I want this internship.

**Mr. Clay**: All right, but have you done any volunteering?

**Yi Fei**: I've been a coordinator for exchange students at the university. Also, my friends and I once launched a campaign, appealing to volunteers to tutor students in rural areas. Does that count?

**Mr. Clay**: Yes, that's excellent. In your resume, you should outline the responsibilities you had and the skills you gained from your volunteering experiences. You also need to try and stand out from other applicants, so it's a good idea to have a personal statement that includes your career goals and is linked to the position you are applying for.

**Yi Fei**: So I should tailor the resume to the job, right?

**Mr. Clay**: Yes, and you can include actual examples from your studies to show you have what the company is looking for.

**Yi Fei**: So something like: "At university I always meet the deadlines for assignments and have used my organizational skills to help other students draw up study plans."

**Mr. Clay**: That's a good start. Oh, remember to write something about your hobbies if they're relevant to the job.

**Yi Fei**: Really? But my hobby is gaming.

**Mr. Clay**: Oh that's OK. You could write: "I'm a keen gamer and in order to improve my scores, I often use data analysis of my performance to know how to improve." Or something like that.

**Yi Fei**: OK, I'll come up with something. Thanks, that's really helpful!

**Mr. Clay**: No problem.

# Unit 4 GIVING BACK TO SOCIETY

## Section 1 · Episode 1 · Video scripts

Tom: Xiaohui, I heard that today's meeting will raise the issue of a new CSR campaign?
Sun Xiaohui: Yes, the last campaign has achieved its objective, and it's time for a new one.
Tom: Oh, OK. So, just to be clear, CSR is basically a form of advertising, right?
Sun Xiaohui: Well, not entirely. I would say it's more a way for a company to show its true values. It's actually really important that the company should truly believe in the issues that the campaigns are devoted to.
Tom: Ah, I see.
Sun Xiaohui: Most companies see it as a chance to give back to the community and wider society in which they live.
Tom: Ah!

Jia Yi: Good morning!
Sun Xiaohui & Tom: Morning!
Jia Yi: So, today I'd like to announce that we will be starting a new CSR campaign, and it will be the focus of our work in the coming weeks. Tom, can you take the minutes so we know what has been decided?
Tom: Of course.
Jia Yi: Do you have any thoughts?
Tom: Yes, I have a question. What do Chinese companies usually support for CSR?
Jia Yi: It depends, but it can be things connected to the environment, or to helping disadvantaged groups ...
Tom: I see, like trying to reduce poverty?
Sun Xiaohui: That's right. In my last company we helped migrant workers travel home for the Spring Festival.
Tom: How do you do that?
Sun Xiaohui: Migrant workers constitute a key part of China's labor force, and most of them work away from their hometowns. They usually return home for the Spring Festival.
