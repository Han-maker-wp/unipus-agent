Identifying the sequence of events in a narrative

The sequence of events is the chronological order in which related events or actions occur. Identifying the sequence of events in a narrative is an important listening strategy that can help you better follow and comprehend a narrative.

## 13 · Taking in · Listening to China · Listening skills

One of the most effective ways to recognize the sequence of events is to listen for the sequencing words or time-order words that are used to connect the various parts of a narrative. Some of these words and expressions also act as signals to indicate whether an event happens in the beginning, in the middle, or toward the end of a narrative.

## 14 · Taking in · Listening to China · Listening skills

## 109 · Speaking out · Role-play · Speaking skills · Giving a tour of a place

Giving a tour of a place is introducing visitors to a place of interest, such as a museum, a historical site, or a natural attraction, allowing visitors to better understand the history, culture, and unique features of the place. There are some steps to follow when you give a tour of a place.

## 110 · Speaking out · Role-play · Speaking skills · Lead the way

You need to give visitors clear and correct explanations as to where they are now, what they can see here, and where they are heading next. You may use spatial prepositions, such as near, over, next to, and under, to explain where it is in relation to other places. You may also use expressions like On the left / right you can see ..., Just around the corner is ..., and ... is directly in front of / behind

## 111 · Speaking out · Role-play · Speaking skills

us to introduce other places or buildings of interest in the surrounding areas. Expressions like In a few minutes, we'll be passing ... and Please follow me, and we will head over to ... are used to show the next location you are heading for.

Talk about facts
When you are showing visitors around a place, you may need to

## 112 · Speaking out · Role-play · Speaking skills

provide facts and figures about its history, architecture, culture, landmarks, significant events, local people, local cuisine, etc., to help the visitors know more about the place they are visiting. When it comes to a specific building, for example, a museum, you may need to introduce the visitors to the information about its geographical position, history, collections, current exhibits, etc.

## 113 · Speaking out · Role-play · Speaking skills · Give comments

During a tour of a place, occasionally, you may make comments on what you see. You may use evaluative expressions, such as famous, best, impressive, worth a visit, and underrated, to show your personal appraisal of what you are introducing. For example, comments like This is one of the best restaurants in the city may make visitors want to have a try.

## 114 · Speaking out · Role-play · Speaking skills

By leading the way, providing facts, and giving comments, you can make a tour both informative and interesting.
