## 05 · Opening up

• This quote implies that the difference between failure and success lies in the level of precision and accuracy with which a task is accomplished. It suggests that doing something "nearly right" is not sufficient; true success is achieved when one strives for perfection and does something "exactly right" by paying attention to details.

• Success is something you achieve after you work hard for it. Therefore, you should have strong determination and make thorough preparations to achieve success despite any difficulties and obstacles that may stand in your way.

## 06 · Opening up

• Being complacent about or satisfied with your current achievements and not striving for improvement can result in failure. On the other hand, being modest and humble, acknowledging your limitations, and constantly seeking growth and self-improvement can lead to success. Therefore, you should work hard while maintaining a low profile, which paves the way to success.

• Success is measured not only by how far you go in life, but also by what you do to get there. A smooth pathway teaches you little about how to face future obstacles, whereas the tough and

## 07 · Opening up

unusual challenges you manage to overcome will give you the strength and confidence to handle potential ups and downs in the future.

Public speaking is the act of making a speech in front of a large group of people in an organized and deliberate manner. Its objective is usually to inform, persuade, or entertain the audience.

## 94 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills · Overall organization of a speech

A good speech should be well-organized, typically consisting of three parts: introduction, body, and conclusion. Each part serves a distinct purpose and function. To ensure a smooth flow of information and a coherent structure, it's important to use proper connectives to bridge these parts and connect ideas within each part.

## 95 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills · Introduction

The introduction prepares the audience for what you are going to talk about in the speech. It needs to accomplish four things:

1 Gain the audience's attention and interest (e.g. by asking questions, telling stories, or citing famous quotes);

## 96 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

2 Establish your credibility on the topic under discussion (e.g. by mentioning your own experiences, knowledge, or skills related to the topic);

3 State the central idea (i.e. the thesis);

4 Preview the main points to be presented in the body.

## 97 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills · Body

The body is the central part of your speech, where you present your central idea in detail. You should do the following:

1 Give two to five main points;

2 Break down each main point into two or more sub-points;

3 Provide specific details.

## 98 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

The main points and sub-points form the skeleton of your speech. To ensure they are clear and persuasive, it's crucial to include specific supporting details, such as concrete examples, engaging stories, and relevant statistics.

Conclusion

The conclusion is the stage where you bring your speech to a

## 99 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

smooth ending. Important things you should do in this part include:

1 Signal the ending (e.g. by using expressions such as In conclusion, Today I have talked about … , and Let me end by saying … );

2 Reinforce the central idea (e.g. by reviewing the thesis and the

## 100 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

main points);

3 Give closing remarks (e.g. by citing famous quotes or referring back to the introduction).

Connectives

Connectives are words, phrases, or sentences that signal a shift from one idea to another and link different parts of your speech.

## 101 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

There are four common types of connectives: transitions, internal previews, internal summaries, and signposts.

1 Transitions are used to indicate a shift from a completed thought to a new one. For example:

Now that we have explored the major causes of noise-induced hearing loss, let us move on to the measures we can take to

## 102 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

prevent it.

2 Internal previews typically come at the beginning of a main point to give an overview of the sub-points. For example:

In discussing how to solve the problem, I will focus on two solutions: building a new campus and promoting distance learning.

## 103 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

3 Internal summaries generally appear at the end of a main point or a set of main points to remind the audience what has been discussed. For example:

Let's sum up what we have discussed so far. First, we examined the background of the war. Second, we discussed its three major stages.

## 104 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

4 Signposts are words or expressions (e.g. ordinal numbers) that tell the audience where you are in the speech. For example:

The first advantage of electric vehicles is that they are energy-efficient.

The second advantage is that they are environmentally friendly.

The third advantage is that they perform better than traditional

## 105 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

gasoline-powered cars, with smoother operation and stronger acceleration.

By organizing your speech with a clear structure and appropriate connectives, you can communicate your ideas logically and effectively. The introduction enables you to introduce the central idea from the start. The body presents your main points with

## 106 · Speaking out · Public speaking · Public speaking skills

relevant supporting details. The conclusion signals the end of your speech and reinforces your central idea. The use of appropriate connectives ensures coherent and smooth transitions between these parts.
