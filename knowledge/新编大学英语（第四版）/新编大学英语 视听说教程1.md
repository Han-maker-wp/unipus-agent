## 1-5 Passage 1 · Thinking and speaking 1

参考：There are two main mindsets we can navigate life with: a fixed mindset and a growth mindset. The given table presents a sharp contrast between them.

Generally speaking, a growth mindset means that intelligence can be developed over time whereas a fixed mindset means that intelligence is static or fixed.

Specifically speaking, these two mindsets differ enormously from each other in how they approach certain circumstances. First, they are at polar opposites in the face of challenges. Those with a fixed mindset are inclined to avoid challenges and bury their head in the sand while those with a growth mindset tend to embrace challenges as a means to develop new skills. Second, those with a fixed mindset are more likely to give up when encountering obstacles. In contrast, those with a growth mindset have the determination to persist in the face of setbacks. Third, their attitudes toward effort are substantially different from each other. Those with a fixed mindset see effort as fruitless or worse while those with a growth mindset view effort as the path to mastery. Fourth, there is a striking contrast in terms of criticism. Those with a fixed mindset are likely to ignore useful negative feedback. However, those with a growth mindset treat criticism as a suggestion for improvement. Fifth, when it comes to the success of others, these two kinds of people also respond differently. Those with a fixed mindset are apt to feel threatened, but those with a growth mindset are capable of finding lessons and inspiration in the success of others. These two markedly different mindsets will definitely produce two results as different as chalk and cheese. Those with a fixed mindset may plateau early and achieve less than their full potential, which confirms their deterministic view of the world, while those with a growth mindset may reach ever-higher levels of achievement, which gives them a greater sense of free will.

"Before that, we had to look through tens of thousands of ears of rice, often while walking barefoot through the paddy field," recalled Mr. Yuan.

It turns out that dream becomes an engine of Mr. Yuan's success.

Second, perspiration. Any scientific breakthrough stems from meticulous and painstaking work. To resolve hard problems, Mr. Yuan and his team had to work in the rice paddy a couple of hours even when the sun was scotching. They had steam buns and drank water from a kettle for lunch. They sweated a lot, but they enjoyed the suffering because they were motivated by a strong sense of hope. While cultivating students, Mr. Yuan also required that every student should work in the rice paddy.

Third, inspiration. The discovery of a new rice variety opened the door to super-hybrid rice breeding. When Mr. Yuan observed new rice varieties in a rice paddy in 1997, a new variety riveted his attention, which gave him a flash of inspiration about an ideal super-hybrid rice type. Mr. Yuan urged researchers to draw inspiration from daily observation and turn it into a reality.

Mr. Yuan's story will provide inspiration to generations far into the future, in China and across the world. I hope that every one of us will cherish the value of dream, perspiration and inspiration. Wish you every success in your study, in your future career, and in life. Thank you.

参考：Procrastination appears to be very common among my classmates. I interviewed six classmates and everyone admitted that they were suffering from procrastination. I also asked them the reasons why they tended to procrastinate. Here are the three causes of procrastination.

1. Vague or abstract goals

When their goals are vague or abstract, they are more likely to procrastinate. For example, goals such as "learn English well" or "stay fit" are relatively vague, too general, and are therefore likely to lead to procrastination. Conversely, goals such as "set aside at least one hour for English study and memorize at least 20 English words on a daily basis" and "go to the gym on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday right after study, and spend at least 30 minutes on the treadmill, running at a high speed" are concrete, and are therefore much more likely to be achieved bit by bit.

2. Indecisiveness

They sometimes procrastinate because they are unable to make decisions in a timely manner. Sometimes, they can't decide which course of action to engage in; at other times they can't make a certain decision before they can move ahead with their general plan of action. For example, they might delay going on a diet, because they can't decide which diet plan to follow. Or they might delay getting started on their research paper, because they can't decide which topic to write about.

3. Avoidance

They sometimes procrastinate because they find a task too challenging or they dislike the task they need to perform. They avoid taking on a challenge out of fear that they won't do the job well. They even feel anxiety, which makes them avoid the task even more. Similarly, when they find a certain task unappealing, they are likely to stay away from it and procrastinate. For example, if they need to make an important phone call to someone they dislike, they might end up procrastinating instead of just getting it done, because they don't want to talk to them.

Literally, it means that when a melon is fully grown, it will fall from the vine by itself. Metaphorically, it means that all things happen when their time comes.

Strike while the iron is hot.

This proverb alludes to the imagery of the blacksmith. If he delays in shaping the iron when it is hot and pliable, the metal will soon cool down and harden and the best opportunity will thus be lost. It suggests that we should take advantage of an opportunity as soon as it arises, in case the opportunity goes away and does not return.

Lose an hour in the morning, and you will spend all day looking for it.

It implies that morning hours are the best time of day. That's why we should make the most of our morning hours. Otherwise, we will be at a loss what to do next.

What flares up fast will extinguish soonest.

It emphasizes that an intense feeling or an all-consuming passion will not last for a long time. Thus, it is advisable that we should value people and the needs of relationships over time.

Time is the master of those who have no other master.

It means that there are far more important things, such as love and friendship, than time in our lives. We can sacrifice our schedules for these things. If not, we may well become the slave of time.

With time and patience, the mulberry leaf will become a silk gown.

Literally, it means that with devotion, care and patience, the mulberry leaf will eventually become a silk gown. The process of making a silk gown goes like this: Mulberry trees are planted; fresh leaves are picked for silkworms; silkworms spin their cocoons; silk threads are unraveled, woven, dyed and embroidered; a silk gown is made. Metaphorically, it highlights that things will take their course with the passage of time. Just as a farmer can never help the seedlings grow by pulling them up, we should be patient enough on our way to success.

Hello, everyone,

Our group has decided to focus on three severe communication problems in the digital age. They are lack of skills to engage in meaningful face-to-face communication, addiction to social media, and cyber bullying. In the following presentation, we are going to briefly describe the problems and then offer some solutions to them.

First, many people nowadays lack the basic skills to have meaningful face-to-face communication. It is not uncommon to see a family or group of friends sitting together without interactions, because everyone is busy looking at their smartphones. Moreover, some people may even feel awkward in a social gathering, if they don't have their smartphones with them. These phenomena reveal a sad fact, that is, some people are simply losing the skills required to carry out meaningful face-to-face communication. To solve this problem, we suggest that everyone try putting their phones away when attending a social gathering, and try truly engaging or interacting with people around them. Remember we all have the ability to have a friendly and interesting conversation and the person next to you is no less interesting than someone you barely know on the Internet. Look in the other person's eyes when you are talking, since this shows you are really interested in what he or she has to say. Try smiling more and say "excuse me" when you have to check your phone. If we all truly make some efforts, we will regain the skills necessary for meaningful face-to-face communication sooner or later.

The second common problem in communication is addiction to social media. It is estimated that over millions of people worldwide suffer from Internet and social media addiction. These people waste a lot of time overthinking and over-planning their posts and seem to forget all other hobbies. They even feel anxious when they cannot check their social media. Clearly, this is not a healthy way of living. Fortunately, there are some things people can do to overcome this addiction. First, addicts can turn off notifications from social media platforms. After posting things, they can also try staying away from their phones for a period of time so that they do not get wrapped up in who is interacting with their post. Second, they should try to place their phone somewhere out of reach before they go to sleep. This way, they will have nothing to do but sleep. Third, they should try to place less weight on their personal social media appearance, because this not only leads to time wasted on overthinking and over-planning their posts, but also puts unnecessary stress on them. Finally, they can try to go back to their old hobbies or cultivate some new ones.

The last problem we want to address is cyber bullying. It can take place on social media, messaging platform and gaming platforms, etc. For example, cyber bullies may spread lies, send hurtful messages or threats, or post embarrassing photos of someone on social media. They may even impersonate someone or do unethical things in their name. Though cyber bullying happens online, it can actually make the victim feel that they are being attacked everywhere, even inside their own home. According to some UNICEF experts, if you think you are being bullied, you should first try to seek help from someone you trust, such as your parents or your teachers. If you feel uncomfortable talking to someone you know, you can search for a helpline in your country to talk to a professional counselor. If the bullying is happening on a social media platform, you should try blocking the bullies and report their behaviors on the platform. You can try collecting evidence, such as text messages and screen shots of social media posts, to show what has been going on. If you feel you are in immediate danger, you should contact emergency services or the police.
